1. Introduction

Municipal services play an important role in maintaining the quality of urban life. Citizens regularly report civic issues such as garbage collection, drainage problems, water supply issues, road maintenance, and sanitation.

This project focuses on analysing municipal citizen grievances in Bengaluru using data associated with Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP).

The study uses descriptive analytics to understand complaint patterns, geographical distribution, reporting frequency, and grievance status.

2. Research Question

What are the patterns and trends in municipal citizen grievances in Bengaluru based on complaint category, geographical distribution, reporting frequency, and grievance status?

3. Research Objectives

  1. To identify the most frequently reported types of municipal grievances.
  2. To analyse the distribution of grievances across different wards.
  3. To examine monthly trends in grievance reporting.
  4. To study the distribution of grievances based on their recorded status.

4. Type of Analytics

This project uses descriptive analytics to summarize historical grievance records using frequencies, percentages, basic statistical summaries, and graphical representations.

6. Dataset Description

The study uses the BBMP Citizen Grievances dataset, containing 1,230,890 records and 12 variables.

The dataset provides information about citizen complaints submitted to the municipal authority in Bengaluru. The variables include complaint category, subcategory, grievance date, zone, ward, grievance mode, grievance status, staff information, and citizen rating.

The dataset provides an opportunity to examine the frequency and distribution of municipal grievances across different categories and geographical areas, along with their reporting patterns and recorded status.

7.2 Date Inspection and Cleaning

# Convert grievance date
raw_data$grievance_date <- as.POSIXct(
  raw_data$grievance_date,
  format = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S",
  tz = "UTC"
)

# Check date range
range(raw_data$grievance_date, na.rm = TRUE)
## [1] "1900-01-01 12:00:00 UTC" "2048-02-28 10:13:00 UTC"
# Create cleaned dataset
clean_data <- raw_data %>%
  filter(
    grievance_date >= as.POSIXct("2020-01-01", tz = "UTC"),
    grievance_date < as.POSIXct("2027-01-01", tz = "UTC")
  )

# Check cleaned dataset dimensions
dim(clean_data)
## [1] 1230416      12

The original dataset contains some unusual dates, including records from 1900 and 2048.

These records are excluded from the initial analysis because they fall outside the selected study period. Their original values are retained in the imported dataset.

8. Geographical Distribution of Grievances

The dataset contains 485 unique ward names across Bengaluru.

The initial ward-wise analysis indicates that Jnanabharathi Ward has the highest number of recorded grievances, with 27,979 complaints, followed by Horamavu with 27,213 complaints.

Rajarajeshwari Nagar, Begur, and Bellandur also appear among the wards with relatively high grievance counts.

#11. Findings and Interpretation

The descriptive analysis of BBMP citizen grievances provides an overview of municipal service requests in Bengaluru.

The analysis examines four major aspects:

Complaint Categories: The category-wise analysis identifies the most frequently reported types of municipal grievances. Monthly Trends: The monthly analysis illustrates changes in grievance reporting over time. Ward Distribution: The geographical analysis identifies wards with higher numbers of recorded grievances. Grievance Status: The status analysis provides an overview of the recorded progress and classification of complaints.

The results help describe the distribution and patterns of municipal grievances across Bengaluru. These findings are based on recorded complaint counts and do not independently measure service quality or municipal performance.

12. Conclusion

This project uses descriptive analytics to study municipal citizen grievances in Bengaluru using the BBMP Citizen Grievances dataset.

Using R programming and graphical visualizations, the study examines complaint categories, monthly reporting trends, ward-wise distribution, and grievance status.

The analysis provides a basic understanding of the types and distribution of civic issues reported by citizens. It also demonstrates how descriptive analytics can help summarize municipal service request data and support better understanding of urban civic concerns.

13. Limitations

The analysis depends on the accuracy and completeness of the available dataset. Some variables contain missing values. Unusual grievance dates were excluded from the selected analysis period. Complaint counts do not necessarily indicate the actual severity of civic problems. The study uses descriptive analytics and does not attempt to predict future complaints.
